Rabbits and foxes

Rabbits breed; foxes eat rabbits, and breed on what they eat; foxes die. That is the whole of the classic predator-prey model, and it is enough to make both populations rise and fall in turn, for ever.

Here it is as a Simile model. The two boxes are the populations, the arrows with valves are flows into and out of them, and the thin arrows show what each rate depends on.

Two variables sit between the populations. eaten_per_fox is how many rabbits one fox eats in a unit of time, here simply proportional to the number of rabbits; rfox is how many young a fox raises on that. Mathematically this is the textbook Lotka-Volterra model, but drawing these two out, rather than leaving them inside the equations for predation and fox_repro, puts the model's real assumptions where they can be seen and argued with.

What it does

The model ran as this page opened, for 50 time units.

Rabbits multiply while foxes are few. Foxes then multiply on the plenty, eat the rabbits down, and starve back. Each peak in foxes comes just after a peak in rabbits.

Try it

Double-click any element of the diagram to see and change its equation — try a lower fox death rate in fox_mortality — and the model runs again with your change. Nothing you do here is kept: Reset model puts it back, and so does coming back to this page.
