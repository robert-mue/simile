Rabbits and foxes

Rabbits breed; foxes eat rabbits, and breed on what they eat; foxes die. That is the whole of the classic predator-prey model, and it is enough to make both populations rise and fall in turn, for ever.

Here it is as a Simile model. The two boxes are the populations, the arrows with valves are flows into and out of them, and the thin arrows show what each rate depends on.

Two variables sit between the populations. eaten_per_fox is how many rabbits one fox eats in a unit of time, here simply proportional to the number of rabbits; rfox is how many young a fox raises on that. Mathematically this is the textbook Lotka-Volterra model, but drawing these two out, rather than leaving them inside the equations for predation and fox_repro, puts the model's real assumptions where they can be seen and argued with.

The six circles on the outside are the model's parameters: numbers that nothing else in the model influences, drawn out of the equations so that each has a name and a place on the diagram. rabbit_birth_rate is young per rabbit per unit of time, attack_rate the share of the rabbits one fox catches, conversion_rate young foxes per rabbit eaten, and fox_death_rate the share of foxes that die in a unit of time. initial_rabbits and initial_foxes are the populations the run starts from.

What it does

The model ran as this page opened, for 50 time units.

Rabbits multiply while foxes are few. Foxes then multiply on the plenty, eat the rabbits down, and starve back. Each peak in foxes comes just after a peak in rabbits.

Try it

Move a slider and the plots above follow it: the model runs again, here in the page, every time the slider moves. The model itself is not changed: model values puts the sliders back.

You can also change the model itself. Double-click any element of the diagram to see and change its equation, and the model runs again with your change. Nothing you do here is kept: Reset model puts it back, and so does coming back to this page.

This model is built step by step in the Predator-prey tutorial (Stage 3); here its constants are drawn out as parameters. The page runs it on SimiLive, the Simile engine at simulistics.com.
